# The analysis panel and floating analysis panels

Two overlays as axes over the quantity cube; sample, brush, paint; the OPERA joint histogram. Plan and phases: `ANALYSIS_TOOL_PLAN.md`.

*Part of the per-feature reference split out of [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) on 2026-11-14 (text moved verbatim). "Above"/"below" references to other sections may now point into a sibling file — the index is in CLAUDE.md.*

**The analysis panel — two overlays as axes, the map as the sampler (`analysis.js` + `ls_sample.js`, 2026-10-10; ANALYSIS_TOOL_PLAN.md phases 1–2).** It replaced the susceptibility scatter and the permafrost density panel, which are its presets (`an=scatter` → lw × n10, `an=pf` → PZI × probability).

- **Quantities.** An overlay can be an axis when `_QUANTITY_OF[ov.id]` in `map.js` names a `key` in the server's cube — `tools/analysis/quantities.py` is the registry (label, units, axis range and bins, ordinal classes, the native raster). 24 today: elevation, slope, aspect, northness; slope at the five other fixed scales (local, 200 m, 500 m, 1 km, 2 km; below); the eight permafrost products and Pastick; lw, n10, DGGS, NASA global; summer coherence; ITS_LIVE speed. **The fixed-scale slopes have no value raster** (the bake kept only colour), so `tools/analysis/slope_scale_values.py` evaluates the bake's own functions (`bake_slope.py`, `chord_slope.py`) on the Mapterhorn z12 tiles on Nunatak at each lattice point and landslide centroid: it writes `data/quantities/slope_<sid>.npy` directly and `data/slope_scales/landslides.json`, which `sample_quantities.py` reads for entries with `points`; registry entries are `computed=True` and the cube builder skips them. 468 z8 blocks, 26 min on 3 workers (2026-11-07). Checked: point evaluation equals the bake's raster functions exactly at 400 pixels per scale; r vs the 60 m Albers `slope` 0.96 (local) to 0.99 (200 m), 0.80 at 2 km. The bake's water mask (flat ground ≤ 5 m) blanks ~28k Alaskan land cells (1.9 %) that `land.npy` keeps. **The 60 m row (`sp-slope`) is still bound to `slope`** (ordinary slope on the 60 m Albers grid), because that axis has a 60 m archive and paints; the true 60 m steepest-line values exist as `slope_60.npy` (and in landslides.json) but are not registered. The five new axes brush but do not paint. Not yet: Hugonnet dh/dt and IceBoost (per-UTM-zone tiles, need a warp first), OPERA (no statewide raster), DIST and geology (categorical groupings, not axes).
- **The cube** (`tools/analysis/build_quantity_cube.py` → `data/quantities/<key>.npy` + `land.npy` + `grid.json`, ~23 MB a quantity, **rsynced to the droplet with `data/`**): every quantity **point-sampled at the centre of each cell of the 1 km EPSG:3338 lattice** (the permafrost grid) — exactly the published value for a 1 km product, a systematic 1-in-278 sample of a 60 m one. Not a mean: a density of 1 km *means* of slope is a smoother field than the one a landslide centroid is sampled from, and the two would be drawn on different distributions. Same-CRS rasters are read in bands and indexed (no overviews, exact); others go through a nearest `WarpedVRT`. The land mask is the 60 m terrain grid's own.
- **Server** (`inventory/analysis.py`): `api/analysis/quantities/` (the registry), `api/analysis/density/?x&y` (the 2-D histogram of every land cell with both values — on demand, ~0.6 s cold for the first pair, cached per process; the ordinal DGGS axis bins by class position), `api/analysis/sample/?lon&lat&r&q=` (the lattice cells inside a circle, columnar). All 503 with a message when `data/quantities/` is absent.
- **Axes = the wiper panes**, bound both ways (Hig's framing). Opening the panel over a wiper whose panes show axis-capable overlays adopts them (left = x, right = y); picking an axis in the panel calls `_anWiperSet`, which creates the wiper on the current basemap if there is none, shows x's overlay left and y's right, and turns **every** other overlay off on both panes. **While the panel is open a pane holds exactly one axis-capable overlay** (Hig, 2026-10-10): non-axis overlays (contours, outlines, DIST, geology) go off when it opens and their rows are locked everywhere (`_overlayRow` `paint()`, dimmed + disabled, resynced by `_leftPanelSync` on open/close); extras are pruned to the first; and an overlay checked in any panel turns the pane's others off before `_anOverlaysChanged()`, so the one just chosen becomes the axis (without that pruning `adoptWiper` kept the first in registry order and a pick in the left panel looked dead). **Choosing the overlay the other pane shows swaps the axes**: the other pane takes this pane's previous overlay (or, if the pane was empty, the overlay of the axis the plot still holds for it) — `adoptWiper` cannot take x = y, and before this the map showed one overlay on both panes while the plot kept the old pair (Hig's "wiring broke", 2026-10-10). The two pane panels list only axis-capable overlays while the panel is open (`_ovRenderGrouped(…, onlyAxes)`). `map.js` calls `LSAnalysis.onOverlays()` from the overlay row checkbox, `_ovApplyHashSpec` and the wiper enable/disable so later changes are adopted while the panel is open; a `syncing` flag stops the loop. **While the panel is open with a wiper, a left-pane control panel** (`_leftPanelSync`, `#wiper-panel-left`, over the map's left side) twins the right-pane one — same basemap cards, same overlay rows (`_overlayRow(ov, 'left', 'lpanel')`: the third argument scopes its `_ovRowSync` entries so they do not overwrite the sidebar's) — because with the sidebar on the Analysis tab the x axis's controls were otherwise off screen (Hig, 2026-10-10). Its ✕ hides it until the panel next opens; the right panel's title reads "· y axis" meanwhile.
- **Two plots on one pair of axes** (Hig, 2026-10-10): *terrain* (the log-blue density, with the sample blotch and the brush) and *landslides* (points; or the old scatter's grid — count per cell or landslides per 1000 km² of terrain, YlOrRd with the off-ramp catch colour, log-binned, legend in the footer; hover names, click opens; `Limit to map view` respected) drawn over a faint (α 0.28) copy of the terrain. The **grid slider** coarsens both together: level k sums 2^k × 2^k of the cube's bins (`coarsen()`, a plain merge, instant), the landslide grid is built on the same merged cells, and the rate is Σcount ÷ Σkm² of the merged cell — i.e. the area-weighted mean of the raw rates with empty raw cells weighing in, never a mean of rates (Hig asked for this check). **The rate's colour scale is absolute** — 0.1 to 1000 per 1000 km², `RATE_LO/RATE_HI`, the same at every level and on every pair of axes; scaled to each level's own maximum it made coarsening *look* like rates rising when the maximum was falling (slope × elevation: 1000 → 91 → 29 → 20 → 6 from 1× to 16×, terrain at ≥ 10/1000 km² 8,004 → 0 km², the thing Hig spotted). Count mode stays scaled to its plot's fullest cell — counts are meant to grow as cells merge. A cell with landslides but no sampled terrain gets the catch colour, not nothing. The top of the slider is where the shorter axis would fall below 4 cells. `gr=<level>` in the URL.
- **Sample** (`LSSample`, `LSTools` mode `sample` with no toolbar button): the panel's ◎ button is a tool — while on, a ghost circle of the chosen size follows the cursor (`ls-sample-ghost`, fill + dashed line; the header's number is the **diameter**, up to 100 km, because "a 20 km sample" reads as 20 km across — `LSSample`, the API and `smp=` keep the radius), each click sets the sample there and the tool stays on; Esc, the button, another tool claiming the map, or closing the panel puts it away **and removes the sample and its highlights with it** — the circle exists only while the tool is on, so a `smp=` arriving by URL arms the tool (Hig, 2026-10-10). Closing the panel also clears the brush rings on the map. Changing the radius re-reads an existing sample. **Subsets (2026-10-31):** the sample and *Limit counts to map view* are the two subsets, exclusive (checking the box — in the panel header (`#an-limit`) or the Inventory tab (`cb-limit-view`), one checkbox: `opts.limitView` sets it and dispatches its change, whose listener calls `LSAnalysis.onLimitView()` — puts the sample away and dims ◎; arming ◎ unchecks it). A subset is whole 1 km cells: `api/analysis/density/?…&view=w,s,e,n` or `&circle=lon,lat,r` returns the subset's histogram, the landslide ids in it (centroid's cell, `analysis._ls_cells`; view test is `makeViewportTest`'s wrap, ported as `_in_view`), per-axis stats and, for a circle, the counted cells' outline (`an-cells` on the map). The terrain pane draws the denominator alone — the subset's terrain when there is one, else the reviewed area's — in discrete log classes (`T_RAMP`, ColorBrewer Blues 7, 1 km² to the plot's fullest cell, Hig 2026-10-31); the landslide pane counts only the subset's landslides over the subset's terrain. The 60 m reading of the sample (`LSSample.fine`) is no longer used by this panel (the permafrost detail still uses it). **The map's filters apply** through `_exprEval` in map.js, a JS evaluator of the very expression `buildFilter` hands the layers (`opts.passes`) — verified against independent counts on six filters; a new op in `buildFilter` must be added there. The permafrost detail opens from here.
- **Brush**: a click on either plot places a small box around it (± `BRUSH_FRAC` = 5 % of each axis — Hig's "default brush", cheap to click around with), a drag makes any box; the landslides inside are ringed on the map. With lw and n10 on the axes it also drives those two sliders (the only quantities with filters), and `buildFilter` → `LSAnalysis.onFilter()` moves the box when the sliders move. A click that lands on a landslide point opens it instead.
- **The brush paints the map** (plan phase 3, built 2026-10-10 evening, reviewed and deployed 2026-10-11): where BOTH axes have a 60 m value archive (`LSSample.FINE`: elevation, slope, aspect, northness, the four permafrost downscales), the ground whose two values fall inside the box is coloured on the map — "where is PZI > 0.6 *and* slope > 30°?" — **in the sample's orange** (map → plot is the orange blotch, plot → map the orange paint; Hig wanted the two directions to match) and **on top of the data stack** on both panes (above overlays and points, under the sample circle and the brush rings; `ensureLayers` moves `an-paint`, the `ls-sample-*` layers and `an-highlight` to the top, called from `initDataLayers` and `_swipeAddData` once each map's stack exists). The `anpaint://<seq>/{z}/{x}/{y}` protocol (`analysis.js paintLoader`) reads the two z10 value tiles through `LSSample.fineTile` (archive read, or slope/aspect/northness derived once per elevation tile and cached) and writes a transparent PNG with the in-box pixels filled; z8–9 tiles are assembled from their z10 children, z11+ cropped from the parent, so the paint shows from z8 (`PAINT_MINZ`). Each rendered tile is cached per sequence and **shared by the two panes** (both maps ask for the same tiles; before that every tile was built twice), and the in-box test is four plain comparisons. A new box bumps the sequence and `setTiles` refetches. The header's **paint map** box turns it off (`pt=0` in the URL); the footer names the box's ranges. Quantities without a fine archive (lw, n10, DGGS, coherence, ITS_LIVE, the 1 km products) brush but don't paint, and the note says so. Cost that remains: a z8 tile needs 16 z10 reads per field, so the first paint at z8 over new ground is the slow case; the z10 decodes are cached across boxes and pans.
- **The reviewed area is the denominator** (2026-10-31, Hig: "assume we have reviewed the relevant area"). PostGIS `reviewed_areas` (`migrate_reviewed_areas`, idempotent, **run once per environment**; seeds one row, Alaska, from the `alaska` region subset). **It grows by adding rows, never by editing one**; the union is the area. `analysis._reviewed()` rasterises the union onto the 1 km lattice at cell centres, re-checks the table (count, max id, max added_at) on every density call and rebuilds only on change. `api/analysis/density/` counts only land cells inside it; `api/analysis/reviewed/` lists the landslides whose centroid's cell is outside (`analysis.js` leaves them out of the landslide plot and says how many: the header count gets a `*` and `#an-note` below the colour keys carries the footnote — outside the reviewed area, unsampled, filtered — with other notices under it, errors red; it used to sit over the landslide plot, moved 2026-11-08, 3dd3f01). No table = no mask (the old behaviour), so deploy order does not matter. Before this the lattice's Yukon/BC/NWT land (831,767 of 2,332,146 km²) was in every denominator: rates were ~2/3 of their value (mean 0.68 → 1.04 per 1000 km²). Adding a row has no UI yet (SQL, or `load_region_geometry`-style command to come). **lw and n10 are `alaska_only`** in `quantities.py`: the USGS rasters write 0 over Canada (98 % zeros on steeper ground), so the cube builder and `sample_quantities.py` blank them outside `data/regions_seed/alaska_3338.geojson` (exported from the `alaska` region; command in `quantities.py`). Deploy: `data/quantities/{lw,n10}.npy` + `grid.json` rsynced to the droplet.
- **`sample_quantities.py <key …>` samples only those keys** (2026-11-07) and carries every other column over from the committed file by id, because the permafrost grids it would otherwise re-read are on R2, not Nunatak (quantities.py reads `PF_BUILD` for a local copy): a missing source would have written a column of nulls. A centroid the file did not have gets only the named keys and is listed in the file's `unsampled: {id: [key…]}`, which the panel counts as missing on those axes (18 records added on prod since 2026-10-31 are in that state; a full run with the permafrost grids restored clears it).
- **`ls_quantities.json` is a committed snapshot** and the panel says when it is stale: landslides present on the map but absent from the file are counted in the note ("N landslides have no sampled values (added since … built 2026-10-10) — tools/analysis/sample_quantities.py --centroids"); the file carries `built`. Sampling at save time would need GDAL in the web container, which it does not have.
- Verified in dev 2026-10-10 by reading the same circle both ways: 60 m medians vs lattice medians agreed (slope 9.9° vs 9.8°, aspect 170° both, elevation 849 m both, northness +0.007 vs +0.010).

**Floating analysis panels.** The seasonal histogram, time-series, the analysis panel, and the **OPERA velocity view-histogram** are floating/draggable/resizable panels via the shared `makeFloatingPanel(panel, {handle, toggle, close, onResize, onChange})` helper in `map.js`. The OPERA panel (`updateOperaHist` + `_opCollectJoint`) is the tile-pyramid payoff: it covers the viewport with BOTH tracks' value tiles at `min(ceil(zoom), 12)` from the same-origin proxy (asc and desc share the 3857 grid, so the same pixel index is the same ground cell), canvas-decodes (LRU-cached ImageData), crops edge tiles to the view (dateline-wrapped x), and accumulates a **2-D joint histogram** — asc velocity on x, desc on y at native 255×255 value resolution, rendered as a log-scaled Blues density with `y=x` / `y=−x` reference diagonals (same-sign = vertical-ish motion; opposite-sign = E–W aspect-driven motion — the aspect-bias read the plot exists for). Footer: both-track coverage, asc/desc-only counts, clipped fraction. Recomputed on debounced moveend with a stale-request token. All four panels (header is the drag handle; `ResizeObserver` redraws; each self-contained — no shared `charts-container` strip). Time-series y-axis is events/month (finest bin granularity). Chart subtitles only say "in view" when "Limit to map view" is checked.

## Slope-aspect stereonet

*Moved here from the state of play 2026-11-14; it was the only description of the feature.*

**Slope-aspect stereonet** (2026-11-10, b3d1906; `stereonet.js`, `#sn-float`,
`an=stereo`, `sn=`/`sb=`/`spt=`). The analysis panel's two plots on a polar
net, reusing its density endpoint (`xbins=288&true_north=1`: 1.25° bins, so
sectors down to 2.5° centre on the compass) and, through `LSAnalysis.shared`,
its scales and map layers. No wiper, no data to deploy. Hig tested it in dev;
on prod the endpoints were checked and the plot drawn once in a browser.
Aspect is true north here and still grid north in the x-y panel and the
aspect overlay (HAZARDS §Raster sources). That panel's `draw()` also still
sizes its canvas before writing its legend, the stretch fixed here.
**Equal-area cells + aspect effect** (2026-11-11, tested in dev by Hig,
deployed the same day): sectors × rings replaced by equal-area cells on the
hemisphere of slope normals (cap + rings of 6k, `cells` select,
`sn=<slope>,e<K>,<rim>,<mode>`, old form still read) and an *aspect effect*
mode (observed ÷ expected from a 2°-smoothed slope rate, key in powers of
two about 1). Sparse cells are drawn, never masked (Hig: go bigger if there
is less data). Net markings black and fine, cell edges white. Offline
comparison of methods: kernel vs. hex grid scored by held-out likelihood,
both ~0.90 nats/landslide vs 0.85 for slope alone.
