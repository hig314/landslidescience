Look-direction epochs do not coincide. Ascending covers {{ rec.track_spans.ascending.0 }}–{{ rec.track_spans.ascending.1 }}; descending covers {{ rec.track_spans.descending.0 }}–{{ rec.track_spans.descending.1 }}. {% if rec.overlap %} They overlap only over {{ rec.overlap.0 }}–{{ rec.overlap.1 }}. {% endif %} A three-dimensional fit that uses each track's full-record rate is therefore combining different epochs, which is safe only if the rate is steady — and the Ω(t) series below show it is not. Each element carries a common-window refit with both tracks restricted to the overlap; where that differs from the full-period value, trust the common-window number for three-dimensional claims and the time series for rate behaviour.

Ω is the rotation rate about the expected gravitational pole — the horizontal axis perpendicular to the element's average dropline. Positive Ω lowers the centre of mass. Because a rotation writes a spatial gradient into the line-of-sight field while a translation writes a constant offset, Ω remains identifiable from a single look direction even where the full three-dimensional motion is not — which is what lets the series span the whole record rather than just the two-track overlap.

How to read these

  • Element boundaries are grown, not assumed. Growth starts from the strongest-signal block and adds neighbours only while each candidate's own residual stays inside the noise tolerance measured on the halo outside the mapped polygon. Blocks outside the polygon joining an element is evidence about the boundary, which may be gradational.
  • Open symbols on the stereonet mean upward motion, and upward motion can be real. A block rotating so that its centre of mass falls will still push part of itself up — a toe rising is the rotational signature, not a data problem. Gravitational consistency is judged by whether the centre of mass lowers, which in the small-motion limit is the mean vertical rate.
  • The period average can be meaningless. Where Ω changes sign inside the record, averaging it produces a number near zero that describes no actual state of the slope. Read the time series first.
  • Single-track points are a control, not a compromise. Where the two tracks overlap, the ascending-alone and descending-alone series are independent estimates of the same quantity; agreement between them is what rules out a look-geometry artefact.
